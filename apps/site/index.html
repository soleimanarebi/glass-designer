About structural glass

Glass is brittle: it does not yield, so its strength depends on surface flaws, on how long a load acts, and on whether the glass has been heat treated. Annealed glass, heat-strengthened glass and thermally toughened glass carry very different stresses, and the same pane is weaker under a long-term load than under a wind gust.

Laminating plies with an interlayer adds safety after breakage, but the plies only partly act together, so the stiffness depends on the interlayer and the load. In a sealed insulating unit the gas in the cavity shares load between the panes and pushes on them as temperature and air pressure change.

Summarised from BS EN 16612:2019 clauses 6 to 9 and Annexes C and D, and the IStructE guide, chapters 3 and 5.

  • 45N/mm², characteristic strength of annealed glass, IStructE Table C.1
  • 70 / 120N/mm², heat-strengthened / toughened float, EN 16612 Table 6
  • 1.0 / 0.29kmod for a wind gust / a 50 year load, IStructE Table C.5
  • L/65deflection limit, or 50 mm, EN 16612 9.1.4; 25 mm for barriers, BS 6180

Why Glass Designer

A glass check pulls factors from several standards at once: strength from one table, duration from another, interlayer behaviour from a third. The usual tools each miss something.

  • Spreadsheets

    Quick for one pane, but load sharing in units and laminate effective thickness get copied and re-copied.

  • General FE software

    Rigorous plate analysis, but the partial factors, kmod and barrier loads are still yours to look up.

  • Hand calculations

    Clear and checkable, but slow to repeat for every pane size, build-up and load combination.

  • Glass Designer

    The standards' factors built in, every value sourced, results as you type, and a report laid out for a checker.

What it does

  • Stress and deflection

    Plate analysis on four edges, beam strips on two, cantilevers clamped at the base, with an optional large-deflection method for uniform loads.

  • Laminates and units

    Effective thickness for each load case from the interlayer, load sharing between the panes and the isochore cavity pressure for summer and winter.

  • Barriers

    BS 6180 line, infill and point loads at the right height, a 25 mm limit for barrier panes, the post-failure check and the handrail rule.

  • Every ply in 3D

    Plies, interlayers and the cavity drawn to scale or exaggerated, coloured by utilisation, stress or deflection for any combination.

  • Working you can audit

    Each check opens to its formula with your numbers in it, and the document, clause and page each value came from.

  • A report for the file

    A PDF with title block, design data, loads, combinations, results per pane and an appendix of working.

Checked against published worked examples

The plate solution reproduces the RWTH Aachen verification values for MEPLA (8.641 mm and 18.71 N/mm² for a 1000 × 2500 × 6 mm pane under 1 kN/m²). The IStructE balustrade and glass wall examples match once their rounding is taken out, and the example IGU report's effective thicknesses (8.42 and 9.45 mm), load sharing and cavity pressures (16.00 and −16.10 kPa) are reproduced. Expected results were computed by a separate script, outside the engine, and each case notes where the printed figures differ and why.

About

Glass Designer is an independent tool for facade engineers, glaziers and fabricators. It applies its authors' reading of BS EN 16612, the IStructE guide Structural use of glass in buildings, BS 6180 and BS 6262-4.

It is not produced, reviewed or endorsed by BSI, CEN or the IStructE. Every design it touches must still be checked and approved by a qualified structural engineer. If something looks wrong, please say so: corrections shape what comes next.
